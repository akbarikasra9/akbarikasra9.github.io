Assignment 7

Overview

Research Question: Do countries that spend more on healthcare per person tend to have longer life expectancies?

For Assignment 7, our team developed an interactive R Shiny dashboard to explore relationships between healthcare spending, life expectancy, and economic development across countries.

Our dashboard includes three visualizations based on Andrew Abela’s Chart Suggestions—A Thought-Starter:

  1. Chart 9: Scatterplot — Relationship between two numeric variables.
  2. Chart 18: Bubble Chart — Relationship among three numeric variables.
  3. Chart 7: Histogram — Distribution of a numeric variable.

These charts allow users to investigate patterns across countries and compare differences in healthcare spending and health outcomes.

Data Source

Dataset: World Development Indicators (WDI)

Source: World Bank

Year: 2023

Website: https://databank.worldbank.org/source/world-development-indicators

We examine the following variables:

Variable Description
Healthcare Expenditure per Capita Healthcare spending per person (current US\() | | Life Expectancy at Birth | Expected years of life at birth | | GDP per Capita | Economic output per person (current US\))
Continent Geographic grouping of countries

The World Development Indicators dataset provides internationally comparable measures of economic development and population health.

Because countries differ in population size, income, and healthcare systems, these variables help us explore whether higher spending corresponds with better health outcomes.

Interactive Dashboard

Our team created and deployed an interactive R Shiny application using Posit Connect Cloud.

The dashboard allows users to explore the data, adjust filters, and examine different relationships through three linked visualizations.

Open the Full Interactive Dashboard

Chart 9: Scatterplot

Healthcare Spending and Life Expectancy

The scatterplot examines the relationship between two quantitative variables:

  • X-axis: Healthcare expenditure per capita (US$).
  • Y-axis: Life expectancy at birth (years).
  • Color: Continent.

Each point represents a country.

What the chart shows:

The visualization allows us to examine whether countries with higher healthcare expenditures tend to have longer life expectancies.

It also helps identify countries that spend substantially more on healthcare without achieving correspondingly higher life expectancy.

The relationship is observational and does not establish that healthcare spending causes longer life expectancy.

Chart 18: Bubble Chart

Healthcare Spending, Life Expectancy, and Economic Development

The bubble chart expands the scatterplot by introducing a third quantitative variable.

  • X-axis: Healthcare expenditure per capita.
  • Y-axis: Life expectancy at birth.
  • Bubble size: GDP per capita.
  • Color: Continent.

Each bubble represents a country.

What the chart shows:

This visualization allows us to explore how economic development relates to healthcare spending and population health.

Larger bubbles represent countries with higher GDP per capita.

By comparing bubble positions and sizes, users can investigate whether wealthier countries also tend to spend more on healthcare and achieve longer life expectancies.

Chart 7: Histogram

Distribution of Healthcare Expenditure

Our team selected Chart 7 from Abela’s Thought-Starter, a histogram, as the third visualization.

The histogram displays the distribution of healthcare expenditure per capita across countries.

  • X-axis: Healthcare expenditure per capita (US$).
  • Y-axis: Number of countries.
  • Grouping: Selected countries or continents.

Why this chart suits our data:

A histogram is appropriate because healthcare expenditure is a continuous numeric variable.

Unlike the scatterplot and bubble chart, which focus on relationships, the histogram highlights how spending levels are distributed across countries.

It helps identify the concentration of countries at different spending levels and whether the distribution is skewed.

Reactivity and Interaction

Our Shiny application includes interactive features that allow users to explore the same dataset from multiple perspectives.

Interactive Controls

Continent selection

Users can select geographic regions to compare countries within or across continents.

Healthcare expenditure filtering

Users can adjust the spending range to focus on countries meeting selected criteria.

Chart-specific controls

Additional controls allow users to explore different chart settings and visual relationships.

Shared Reactive Data

The dashboard uses R Shiny’s reactive() functionality to maintain a common filtered dataset across multiple charts.

When users change the shared filters, the relevant visualizations update together.

This makes comparisons more consistent because the charts reflect the same selected observations.

Interactive Visualizations

Interactive chart features help users explore individual observations and understand differences among countries.

These interactions make the dashboard more useful than a collection of static charts.

Key Takeaways

Our dashboard demonstrates how different visualization techniques answer complementary questions.

Scatterplot: Examines the relationship between healthcare spending and life expectancy.

Bubble chart: Adds GDP per capita to investigate the role of economic development.

Histogram: Examines the distribution of healthcare spending across countries.

Together, these visualizations help users investigate global differences in healthcare investment, economic conditions, and health outcomes.

The dashboard supports exploratory analysis rather than causal conclusions.

Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the visualizations:

  • Correlation is not causation: Higher healthcare spending does not necessarily cause longer life expectancy.
  • Other influences: Education, public health infrastructure, inequality, and healthcare accessibility may affect outcomes.
  • Cross-sectional data: A single year cannot establish how these relationships change over time.
  • Missing observations: Countries without complete data may be excluded from some comparisons.
  • National averages: Country-level measures may conceal substantial inequalities within countries.

Teamwork and Development

The project brought together several components:

  1. Obtaining and preparing World Bank data.
  2. Developing the required charts using R.
  3. Building reactive controls in Shiny.
  4. Integrating the visualizations into one dashboard.
  5. Deploying the application through Posit Connect Cloud.
  6. Publishing the dashboard on our project website.
