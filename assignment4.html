Interpretation

The output shows both similarities and differences over time among presidents in inaugural speeches. An important similarity is that presidents repeatedly use national and civic themes, especially terms related to America, freedom, safety, and the American people. The comparison of Eisenhower, Johnson, and Nixon shows that presidents use different language to define national priorities. The Bush-Obama keyness comparison also demonstrates that presidents may discuss similar broad national themes while placing different emphasis on particular words and issues. Overall, the results suggest continuity in the ceremonial purpose of inaugural speeches while also showing differences in language, tone, and policy framing.

Wordfish Analysis of U.S. Department of Defense China Reports

Wordfish is a Poisson scaling model that estimates one-dimensional document positions using maximum likelihood. The estimated positions of documents and words can then be plotted to identify systematic differences in language across documents.

This analysis applies Wordfish and related text-analysis techniques to U.S. Department of Defense China Military Power Reports.

Read the Reports Safely

read_pdf_safe <- function(filepath) {
  
  tryCatch(
    {
      pages <- suppressWarnings(
        pdftools::pdf_text(filepath)
      )
      
      text <- paste(
        pages,
        collapse = "\n"
      )
      
      if (
        is.null(text) ||
        length(text) == 0 ||
        nchar(trimws(text)) == 0
      ) {
        warning(
          "No usable text extracted from: ",
          basename(filepath)
        )
        
        return(NA_character_)
      }
      
      return(text)
    },
    
    error = function(e) {
      warning(
        "Failed to read: ",
        basename(filepath),
        " — ",
        e$message
      )
      
      return(NA_character_)
    }
  )
}

Overall Interpretation

The inaugural-speech analysis demonstrates how text-analysis methods can identify both continuity and variation in presidential language. Presidents repeatedly rely on broad national themes, while frequency and keyness analyses reveal differences in rhetorical emphasis across administrations.

The Department of Defense analysis extends these methods to a longitudinal policy corpus. Frequency trends show how attention to issues such as Taiwan, nuclear capabilities, cyber operations, missiles, space, and artificial intelligence changes over time. Keyness identifies vocabulary that is unusually associated with particular administrations. Wordfish estimates an underlying dimension of textual change across reports, while correspondence analysis provides an additional view of similarities and differences among documents. Topic modeling identifies recurring clusters of language, and sentiment analysis examines changes in positive and negative language across the report series.
